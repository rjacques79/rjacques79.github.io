## Map 1- COVID-19 Map Case Rates and Population Density in New York City

https://www.arcgis.com/apps/mapviewer/index.html?webmap=83da07e9750a461393fb6b33eab79e3c
A map of New York City showing geographic differences in COVID-19 case rates and population density. Blue shades represent COVID-19 case rates, while a separate color gradient represents population density across smaller geographic areas.

*Interactive version, live as of October 2026:https://www.arcgis.com/apps/mapviewer/index.html?webmap=83da07e9750a461393fb6b33eab79e3c

**Question:** How do COVID-19 case rates and population density vary across different areas of New York City?

**Data:** The map includes COVID-19 case counts, COVID-19 case rates, and population density.

**Method:** I used ArcGIS Pro to organize and display geographic data related to COVID-19 cases, case rates, and population density. I applied different color schemes to the rate and density layers to help distinguish the two variables and shared the map through ArcGIS Online Map Viewer for public access.

**A design choice I made and why:** I used blue shades to represent COVID-19 case rates and a contrasting color gradient for population density. This helps readers distinguish between the two variables and explore how their geographic patterns differ.

**A limitation of this map:** The map shows geographic patterns but does not establish that population density causes higher COVID-19 case rates. The patterns may also be affected by differences in testing, reporting, and the time period represented by the data. The map alone cannot explain why rates differ between areas.

## Map 2- ## Mapping the 2018 Kīlauea Volcanic Eruption and Lava Flow Extent

**Image description:** A map of the lower Puna region on Hawaii Island showing the area affected by the 2018 Kīlauea volcanic eruption. Satellite imagery from different dates in 2018 is displayed within a red outline marking the area of interest. Nearby communities and geographic landmarks provide context for the mapped area.

**Question:** How did the 2018 Kīlauea volcanic eruption affect the landscape within the designated area of interest on Hawaiʻi Island?

**Data:** The map uses Landsat 8 satellite imagery from July 17, August 2, and August 26, 2018, as well as February 26, 2019. The imagery is displayed using Landsat spectral bands. The map also includes a lava flow extent layer for the 2018 Kīlauea eruption and a red outline identifying the area of interest. The original data publisher and download source for the lava flow extent layer should be confirmed from the layer's metadata.

**Method:** I used ArcGIS Pro to organize and display Landsat 8 satellite imagery from multiple dates to examine changes in the landscape during and after the 2018 eruption. I added the lava flow extent layer and outlined an area of interest to focus the map on the affected region. I also used selected spectral bands to display the satellite imagery and provide geographic context for comparing the landscape over time.

**A design choice I made and why:** I used a bold red outline to mark the area of interest so readers can easily identify the geographic area being examined. I also included imagery from multiple dates to support comparisons of landscape conditions during and after the eruption.

**A limitation of this map:** Satellite imagery can help reveal changes in the landscape, but the appearance of the imagery depends on the selected spectral bands, image dates, and viewing conditions. The map alone does not quantify the total area or volume of lava, determine the full extent of damage to buildings and infrastructure, or explain the eruption's impacts on residents. Additional data and analysis would be needed to answer those questions.
